# Go-to-market and economics

## Commercial thesis

This is a trust-led vertical SaaS sale disguised as a simple consumer-like experience. The product may be self-serve after activation, but early adoption will depend on local proof, migration help, CA confidence, and visible collection outcomes.

The commercial unit is a **business/GSTIN workflow**, not an individual chat user. Price for value and support burden, while keeping messaging, payment-provider, and compliance usage transparent.

## Launch sequence

### Phase 1: founder-led discovery

- one city;
- three candidate cohorts, 10 interviews each;
- five live workflow observations;
- two CA/bookkeeper partners;
- one local trade group or cluster anchor;
- no paid advertising.

Recommended candidate cohorts:

1. repair/maintenance/installation contractors;
2. creative/digital/professional agencies;
3. electrical/hardware/building-material B2B traders with moderate invoice complexity.

### Phase 2: concierge pilot

- 10 qualified businesses;
- white-glove setup and data import;
- founder observes first invoice and first collection workflow;
- weekly review with actual metrics;
- prototype can use manual operations behind the scenes, but financial records and user consent must remain real and safe;
- charge a refundable ₹499–₹999 pilot commitment or require a signed success-to-paid agreement to filter curiosity.

### Phase 3: repeatable cluster launch

- 50 paid businesses in one segment/city;
- one onboarding playbook;
- one language mix;
- two reference customers with measured outcomes;
- five CA/bookkeeper referral partners;
- local webinars/demos and WhatsApp referrals;
- only then test paid lead generation.

### Phase 4: adjacent expansion

Expand one axis at a time:

- new city with same vertical; or
- adjacent vertical in same city; or
- additional language in proven workflows.

Do not change geography, vertical, channel, and product depth simultaneously.

## Core offer

### Outcome-based pitch

> “Invoice banane aur payment follow-up ka kaam 1 minute mein. Kitna paisa pending hai, turant pata chalega.”

Demonstration order:

1. speak one real invoice;
2. show exact draft and PDF;
3. show UPI payment action;
4. ask “Rahul ka kitna pending hai?”;
5. generate a polite reminder;
6. show CA export.

Do not lead with AI, CRM, ERP, analytics, or model names.

## Distribution channels

### 1. CAs, GST practitioners, and bookkeepers

Why they can work:

- trusted adviser;
- recurring access to target businesses;
- direct pain from incomplete/inconsistent data;
- natural onboarding and support layer;
- high referral density.

Partner proposition:

- free CA console/read-only access;
- exception and completeness report;
- standard export;
- client setup fee or 15–25% first-year revenue share;
- co-branded training;
- no attempt to disintermediate tax work.

Guardrail: prevent a low-quality reseller channel that promises filing, loans, or guaranteed compliance beyond the product.

### 2. Trade clusters and associations

The Government/WEF AI playbook recommends cluster-specific adoption because businesses share workflows and trust networks. Use:

- live local-language demonstrations;
- vertical templates and units;
- peer champions;
- group onboarding days;
- association pricing without lifetime discounts.

### 3. Anchor-supplier network

A distributor/manufacturer may sponsor or recommend the assistant to dealers/contractors because cleaner invoices and payment records improve ordering and collections. This can reduce CAC, but data boundaries must be explicit: the anchor cannot see dealer/customer books without specific authorisation.

### 4. User referrals

Referral trigger should follow delivered value:

- first payment recorded;
- first overdue amount collected;
- first CA export accepted.

Offer account credit, not cash that attracts low-quality leads.

### 5. Digital acquisition

Later tests:

- YouTube demos in target language;
- search around “GST invoice WhatsApp”, “payment reminder”, and vertical terms;
- Click-to-WhatsApp lead ads;
- CA/creator partnerships.

Paid channels are viable only when 90-day gross profit after support comfortably exceeds CAC payback targets.

## Pricing experiments

### Test grid

| Test | Variant A | Variant B | What it learns |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Core plan | ₹399/month | ₹499/month | price sensitivity vs support margin |
| Billing period | monthly | ₹4,499/year | commitment and cash conversion |
| Trial | 14 days | first 10 invoices | time vs value-based activation |
| Automation | included cap | ₹199 add-on + usage | willingness to pay for scheduled follow-up |
| CA onboarding | free | ₹999 setup | whether paid setup improves activation |

Do not advertise “unlimited AI” or “unlimited WhatsApp.” Use fair-use limits and pass through exceptional messaging/provider usage transparently.

### Recommended initial packaging

**Collect — ₹499/month or ₹4,999/year**

- one business/GSTIN;
- owner + read-only CA;
- up to 300 invoices/month;
- text and reasonable voice usage;
- PDF and direct UPI QR;
- receivables/partial payments;
- reminder drafts/manual sharing;
- weekly summary;
- CA export.

**Business — ₹999/month or ₹9,999/year**

- three users;
- recurring invoices and imports;
- connected payment provider;
- automated WhatsApp reminder allowance, subject to consent/platform fees;
- advanced roles and reports;
- priority support.

Avoid a permanent broad free plan until organic activation is understood. A free invoice generator attracts users with very low retention and makes support economics opaque.

## Unit economics model

### Illustrative per-account monthly model at ₹499

These are planning assumptions, not quoted vendor rates.

| Cost | Lean self-serve | Assisted early stage |
|---|---:|---:|
| AI text extraction/explanation | ₹5–₹20 | ₹10–₹30 |
| Indic voice | ₹5–₹15 | ₹10–₹25 |
| WhatsApp/platform messages | ₹10–₹40 | ₹20–₹60 |
| PDF/storage/compute/monitoring | ₹15–₹35 | ₹20–₹45 |
| Payment reconciliation | merchant-paid/pass-through | merchant-paid/pass-through |
| Support/onboarding amortised | ₹25–₹60 | ₹100–₹250 |
| Total cost to serve | ₹60–₹170 | ₹160–₹410 |
| Gross margin | 66–88% | 18–68% |

The model works only if ongoing human support falls quickly or is delivered through paid setup/partners. The largest risk is not tokens; it is activation and support.

### Payment cost warning

Do not include payment gateway percentage charges inside ₹499. At public rates near 2%, a merchant collecting ₹5 lakh/month would incur roughly ₹10,000 before tax. Direct UPI/manual reconciliation must remain available, and automated provider fees must be contracted directly by or clearly passed to the merchant.

### CAC and payback targets

At ₹499/month and 75% steady-state gross margin, monthly gross profit is about ₹374. Target:

- partner/referral CAC: ≤₹1,500;
- blended CAC after repeatability: ≤₹2,500;
- CAC payback: <6 months preferred, <9 months maximum;
- 12-month logo retention: >70% for annualising confidence;
- annual prepay share: >40% after product-market fit;
- referral-driven new accounts: >25%.

Paid acquisition at CAC above ₹4,000 is dangerous unless ARPA expands through users, automation, compliance, or financial-service referral revenue.

## 12-month operating plan

### Months 0–2: validate

- 30 interviews;
- 5 workflow observations;
- 2 CA partners;
- policy/legal reviews;
- prototype;
- select one ICP and 10 pilots.

**Spend bias:** founder time, design/prototyping, legal/CA review; minimal engineering.

### Months 2–4: concierge pilot

- 10 businesses;
- 200+ real invoices target;
- measure support, corrections, reminder usage, and payment outcomes;
- establish willingness to pay;
- build only missing critical workflow pieces.

**Gate:** six paying conversions and no critical financial accuracy issue.

### Months 4–7: paid MVP

- 50 paid businesses;
- payment-provider BYO integration;
- CA export accepted by real practitioners;
- first two case studies;
- standard onboarding under 30 minutes;
- support below 20 minutes/account/month after activation.

### Months 7–10: cluster repeatability

- 200 paid businesses;
- 5–10 active referral partners;
- merchant WhatsApp automation pilot for a subset;
- cohort retention and collection-outcome evidence;
- gross margin above 65% excluding founder labour.

### Months 10–12: scale decision

- target 500 paid businesses only if retention supports it;
- decide next vertical/city;
- consider seed capital only if activation, retention, and channel economics are repeatable;
- otherwise deepen product/ICP rather than buy growth.

## Team plan

### Discovery/pilot team

- founder/product lead with field access;
- one strong full-stack engineer;
- part-time product designer/researcher;
- retained CA/GST practitioner;
- retained privacy/fintech/platform counsel;
- customer operations generalist during pilot.

### Post-pilot team

- 2–3 product/backend engineers;
- one frontend/PWA engineer;
- one applied AI/quality engineer (may initially be a backend engineer’s role);
- one product designer/researcher;
- one partner/onboarding lead;
- fractional security/compliance support.

Do not hire a large ML team. The differentiator is workflow/data quality and distribution, not training a foundation model.

## Sales qualification

Score each lead 0–2 on:

- invoice volume;
- repeat customers;
- current outstanding value;
- owner involvement;
- WhatsApp dependence;
- dissatisfaction with current workflow;
- migration complexity;
- willingness to share samples;
- willingness to pay;
- CA/peer referral potential.

Pilot only leads scoring at least 14/20 and without a deeply embedded ERP/POS requirement.

## Case-study metrics

Credible proof should show:

- median time to generate/send invoice before and after;
- percentage of invoices entered on the same day as sale;
- overdue value and aging before/after over a stable cohort;
- reminder send rate and payment within 3/7 days;
- hours saved preparing CA data;
- number of corrections/duplicate customers;
- user quote with exact context.

Never claim the product “increased profit” without a rigorous causal basis.

## Financing strategy

### Bootstrap through validation

The initial product can be validated cheaply. External funding before proof may encourage a broad feature race. Bootstrap or use a small founder/pre-seed budget through 50 paid customers.

### Raise when these are true

- clear ICP;
- 200+ paying businesses or unusually strong contracted distribution;
- 3-month logo retention >80% in mature cohorts;
- CAC payback model <9 months;
- weekly workflow depth, not just invoice generation;
- measurable receivables outcome;
- WhatsApp/platform route contractually understood;
- no unresolved critical compliance architecture issue.

### Capital use after proof

- vertical product depth;
- partner distribution;
- migration/connectors;
- security/compliance maturity;
- multilingual quality;
- working-capital partnerships through regulated institutions.

Avoid subsidising payment fees or permanently free human onboarding.

## Expansion economics

Potential ARPA expansion:

- staff seats and permissions;
- automated WhatsApp utility messages;
- connected payments/reconciliation;
- e-invoice/e-way usage;
- multi-GSTIN/multi-location;
- purchase and inventory modules;
- CA managed-service bundle;
- premium vertical packs;
- lawful referral revenue from regulated finance/insurance partners.

Financial-service referrals should follow, not lead, the product. Consent must be specific and ledger data must never be sold or shared for underwriting without a clear legal basis and merchant authorisation.

## Stop conditions

Pause the current thesis if:

- users generate one-off invoices but keep receivables elsewhere;
- weekly retention after novelty is below 50%;
- support remains above 45 minutes/account/month at ₹499;
- users require POS/inventory before they will pay;
- automated WhatsApp onboarding succeeds for too few merchants;
- users refuse direct UPI/manual marking but reject provider fees;
- CAs see exports as extra cleanup rather than improvement;
- accuracy requires so much confirmation that chat is slower than a form.

Each condition suggests a specific pivot: collection overlay on existing software, a vertical POS product, higher-priced assisted service, or a PWA-first workflow.
